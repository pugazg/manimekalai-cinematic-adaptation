# Chapter 1 Visual Reference Package

Status: **AUTHORING BASELINE — approved for neutral reference studies, not final page art**  
Date: 2026-08-13  
Script basis: `script.md` v0.3  
Editorial gate: `EDITORIAL_AUDIT_v0.3.md`  
Production language: English-first; artwork contains no generated text

## Purpose

This package defines what must be visually stable before any finished Chapter 1 comic page is rendered.

Reference studies are for **continuity and evidence control**, not for publication. They should answer:

- what Puhar feels like at human height;
- which material objects recur across the eight pages;
- how the festival's registered Chapter 1 details remain visible;
- how cosmopolitanism is shown without racial or costume caricature;
- how philosophical plurality is staged without later sectarian shorthand;
- how Madhavi and the partially seen Manimekalai remain consistent when Page 8 is rendered.

Every generated/commissioned study remains `[INTERPRETATION]`.

# VR-01 — PUHAR RIVER–SEA ENVIRONMENT

## Required views

Create one neutral environment board containing separate reference views for:

1. river/sea working edge before dawn;
2. modest working harbour / landing relationship;
3. street leading inland from the maritime zone;
4. public route before civic renewal;
5. same route after fresh sand is spread;
6. performance-quarter exterior suitable for the Chapter 1 → Chapter 2 handoff.

## Evidence basis

- `EV-0007` `[HISTORY]` — Poompuhar identified as a Sangam-age Chola port at the Kaveri-sea confluence;
- `EV-0008` `[HISTORY]` — excavation context;
- `EV-0010` `[INTERPRETATION]` — timber-brick-plaster river-sea design direction;
- `EV-0002` `[TEXT]` — old sand removed / fresh sand spread / public decoration.

## Must communicate

- humid coastal/river environment;
- a city of work and movement rather than monumental emptiness;
- multiple practical routes instead of one implausibly perfect ceremonial boulevard;
- materials that can show wear, repair, mud, sand, water and maintenance.

## Reject if

- a huge later-period gopuram becomes the automatic skyline anchor;
- the city reads as Mughal/Rajput/medieval North Indian;
- the harbour is a modern stone quay or fantasy mega-port;
- every surface looks newly built and pristine;
- exact geography is presented as established fact.

# VR-04 — COSMOPOLITAN PUHAR CROWD SYSTEM

## Narrative function

Support Page 4 and the Page 7 reveal.

`EV-0005` says visitors and speakers of many languages participate in the city. The art must show plurality without pretending we know the exact ethnicity, national costume or facial type of every visitor.

## Crowd design rule

Build variation primarily through:

- occupation;
- travel condition;
- carried goods;
- grooming;
- textile weight/drape;
- jewellery amount/material;
- weathering;
- age;
- social interaction;
- movement through the city.

Do **not** build diversity primarily through exaggerated skin-tone coding, fantasy foreign costumes or modern national stereotypes.

## Required reference groups

- local harbour workers;
- fish/river/coastal workers;
- traders and assistants;
- travellers newly arrived by water;
- travellers arriving from inland;
- flower/performance workers;
- civic messengers/guards;
- teachers/students;
- families/children;
- older residents.

## Visual continuity

Create at least six reusable background individuals who can recur across Pages 1–7 so the city feels inhabited rather than regenerated from scratch each page.

# VR-06 — MADHAVI FIRST-REVEAL CHARACTER SHEET

## Chapter 1 use

Madhavi appears only on Page 8. The reference sheet must support stillness, history and restraint rather than costume spectacle.

## Evidence basis

- `EV-0163` `[CROSS]` — trained and publicly recognised artist;
- `EV-0164` `[TEXT]` — turns toward renunciation after Kovalan's death;
- `EV-0166` `[TEXT]` — withdrawal becomes public knowledge;
- `EV-0174` `[INTERPRETATION]` — body retains traces of artistic training;
- `EV-0178` `[CAUTION]` — do not destroy artistic objects as shorthand for renunciation.

## Required views

- front neutral;
- 3/4 neutral;
- profile;
- seated Page-8 posture;
- hands at rest;
- restrained expression set: listening / contained grief / decision / concern.

## Costume direction

Plain relative to earlier public performance life, but not coded as punishment, dirtiness or loss of human vitality.

No unverified modern monastic uniform.

## Body-language direction

- strong balance;
- controlled shoulders and breath;
- stillness that suggests training, not collapse;
- no constant bowed-head grief pose.

## Reject if

- she looks like a repentant 'fallen woman';
- design copies modern Bharatanatyam costume as biography shorthand;
- renunciation is represented by shaved head/saffron/robes without evidence clearance;
- glamour lighting contradicts the scene's interior restraint.
