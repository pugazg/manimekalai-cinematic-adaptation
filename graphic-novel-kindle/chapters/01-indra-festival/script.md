# Pilot editorial notes

## Editorial change from v0.2

The first English draft leaned too heavily on downstream screenplay invention, especially the guarded-cistern/governance sequence, while omitting several registered Chapter 1 features.

Version 0.3 restores the classical canto's stronger visible spine:

- twenty-eight-day festival (`EV-0001`);
- removal of old sand / spreading of fresh sand and civic decoration (`EV-0002`);
- festival linked with rain, prosperity and relief from famine, sickness and enmity (`EV-0003`);
- teachers and philosophers of multiple paths gathering for debate (`EV-0004`);
- visitors and speakers of many languages in Puhar (`EV-0005`).

The labour-centred opening and distribution/fairness emphasis remain deliberate graphic-novel interpretation rather than disguised source claims.

## What this chapter is doing

The canto establishes Puhar through proclamation, civic renewal, plurality, arrivals and festival expectation. The graphic novel expands the physical work of making that public world so the spectacle has a material foundation before Chapter 2 turns toward Madhavi, Manimekalai and the city's judgement of their absence.

## Bharathidasan flavour used here

`GN-VENPA-USE-001 | Chapter 1 | labour, distribution and public responsibility beneath festival splendour | THEMATIC | no direct quotation`

The Bharathidasan perspective is carried by emphasis, not by imitation of his diction.

## English style target

- concise literary English;
- narration that earns its place beside the art;
- no faux-archaic diction;
- no contemporary American banter;
- no worker used merely as a mouthpiece for the adaptation's politics;
- social meaning expressed through objects, actions and juxtaposition before slogans;
- culturally specific details retained when the image can teach them.
