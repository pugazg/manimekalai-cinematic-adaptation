# Visual and Writing Bible

## Core thesis

The graphic novel should move from **being watched and assigned by others** toward **seeing causes, choosing responsibility and releasing possession**.

The book is not a fantasy spectacle with Buddhist decoration. It is a human, social and philosophical epic in which sacred events alter the story without cancelling material reality.

## Bharathidasan flavour

Use Bharathidasan's registered interpretive emphases as a creative lens, not as an excuse for imitation-by-quotation.

Desired qualities in new Tamil writing:

- concise, muscular sentences;
- lyrical contrasts built from ordinary objects;
- moral clarity without sermonising every panel;
- sympathy for workers and people who are hungry, displaced, imprisoned or socially assigned;
- women stating decisions directly;
- questions that turn private feeling toward public responsibility;
- beauty that is allowed to be beautiful without being treated as consent;
- philosophical language that eventually returns to lived consequences.

### Caption rhythm

Prefer:

> நகரம் விழிக்கவில்லை. அதை விழிக்கச் செய்த கைகள் முதலில் விழித்திருந்தன.

over long explanatory narration.

Prefer:

> விழா அனைவருக்குமானது என்றார்கள். தண்ணீர் எல்லோருக்கும் கிடைத்ததா?

when a social question can be carried by image + one line.

These are newly written project lines, not quotations from Bharathidasan or the classical epic.

## Dialogue policy

Dialogue should sound performable in contemporary-readable literary Tamil without relocating the characters into modern Chennai speech.

- avoid faux-archaic density merely to sound ancient;
- avoid current slang that breaks the historical world;
- retain culturally specific nouns when clarity survives context;
- let powerful characters speak economically;
- let ordinary workers have intelligence, humour and observation;
- avoid using exposition to repeat what the art already shows.

## Manimekalai

### Visual identity

At the beginning:

- young adult Tamil woman;
- trained physical poise inherited from an artistic household;
- observant rather than passive;
- adornment progressively simplified by choice, not as a visual punishment for beauty;
- expressive eyes used for listening and assessment, not constant sorrow.

### Arc in framing

Part I: frequently framed through lattices, crowds, reflective surfaces and other people's sightlines.

Parts II–III: more solitary horizons and direct frontal decisions.

Parts IV–V: obstructed frames, doors and custody geometry.

Parts VI–VII: wider relational frames in which she sees networks of people, institutions, food and consequence.

## Madhavi

Never design her as a fallen-world stereotype.

She is:

- an accomplished artist;
- a worker within a prestige economy;
- a mother carrying grief and responsibility;
- someone whose renunciation does not erase skill, memory or economic consequences.

Her costumes may simplify, but the body still carries years of dance discipline.

## Chitrapathi

Avoid villain shorthand.

She understands:

- household survival;
- patronage;
- public reputation;
- the economic value and danger of artistic visibility.

The conflict is strongest when the reader can understand what she fears losing even while rejecting any attempt to assign Manimekalai's future.

## Sudhamathi

Design for practical intelligence and protective attention.

She should often notice danger or social movement one beat before others. Her body language is active: positioning, watching exits, interrupting trajectories, guiding without speaking over Manimekalai.

## Udayakumaran

He must be visually charismatic enough that the tragedy cannot be dismissed as obvious villainy.

Rules:

- desire is sincere;
- entitlement is still wrong;
- refusal is not flirtation;
- previous-life or karmic connection never becomes present-tense consent;
- his deterioration should be shown through choices, not grotesque visual coding.

## Puhar visual language

### Palette direction

- humid gold at dawn;
- indigo shade;
- dyed textile colour;
- wet timber, rope, grain, flower colour and river-silt neutrals;
- no generic fantasy glow.

### City rule

Every spectacular establishing image must contain visible evidence of labour.

If a procession is beautiful, someone prepared the street.  
If garlands hang, someone strung them.  
If ships carry wealth, someone repairs hulls and lifts cargo.  
If music sounds, someone trained, tuned and is paid—or waits to be paid.

## Sacred events

Sacred events may alter:

- continuity;
- scale;
- sound;
- horizon;
- repetition;
- reflections;
- memory order.

They should not rely on decorative beams of light as proof of holiness.

## Amudhasurabhi

Do not give the bowl a permanent magical aura.

Its extraordinary nature is discovered by consequence:

- food continues to be served;
- recipients remain individuals;
- serving labour continues;
- water, cleanliness, vessels, access and crowd management still matter;
- the miracle increases responsibility rather than removing logistics.

## Violence

Violence should be fast, legible and consequential.

Do not aestheticise Udayakumaran's death as romantic destiny. The panel after violence matters more than the strike: witness, blood, shock, misunderstanding and the state response.

## Philosophy pages

Use spatial design to differentiate arguments, not moral colour coding.

Examples:

- one school may reason through categorised objects;
- another through debate circles;
- another through ritual action;
- another through inference diagrams or teaching gestures.

All speakers should appear intellectually serious until their claims are tested.

## Panel grammar

### Standard page

- 4–5 panels;
- one visual anchor panel;
- one reaction panel;
- one consequence or transition panel.

### Splash page

Use only when the reader's understanding of scale changes.

Good uses:

- first full Puhar reveal;
- first Manipallavam reveal;
- first successful mass feeding;
- city-level famine contrast;
- final visual relinquishment.

### Close-up rule

A close-up should reveal a decision, recognition or withheld response—not merely decorate beauty.

## Tamil lettering

### Balloons

- short clauses;
- ideally 1–3 sentences;
- maximum about 35–45 Tamil words only in exceptional speeches;
- break philosophical argument across actions and reactions.

### Captions

Use captions for:

- time/place transitions;
- compressed classical narration;
- Bharathidasan-flavoured social contrast;
- interior reflection that cannot be drawn.

Avoid captions that describe the picture literally.

## Graphic continuity IDs

Use stable IDs from the pilot onward:

- `GN-CH01-P001` — page;
- `GN-CH01-P001-A` — panel;
- `GN-ASSET-0001` — artwork/reference asset;
- `GN-VENPA-USE-001` — perspective use;
- `GN-RIGHTS-001` — rights question;
- `GN-QA-001` — Kindle/lettering issue.

## Evidence footer during production

Working scripts may include production-only evidence notes below a page. These notes are never printed inside the story page.

Example:

`TRACE: primary canto 1 | repo FU-001/FU-002 staging reference | PERSPECTIVE VENPA-BHARATHIDASAN: THEMATIC | classification [INTERPRETATION]`

## Prohibited shortcuts

- no generic North-Indian palace aesthetic substituted for evidence;
- no serpent/reptile racialisation of Naga identity;
- no modern saffron-versus-Buddhist colour binary;
- no divine glow as the default sign of goodness;
- no erotic framing that contradicts the story's consent logic;
- no crowd of identical anonymous poor people around the magic bowl;
- no text-heavy philosophy pages that become illustrated essays;
- no claim that generated architecture or costume is 'historically exact.'
