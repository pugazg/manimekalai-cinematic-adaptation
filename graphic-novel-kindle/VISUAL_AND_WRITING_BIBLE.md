# Visual and Writing Bible

## Core thesis

The graphic novel should move from **being watched and assigned by others** toward **seeing causes, choosing responsibility and releasing possession**.

The book is not a fantasy spectacle with Buddhist decoration. It is a human, social and philosophical epic in which sacred events alter the story without cancelling material reality.

## Current language policy

**English is the active production language.**

All current scripts, captions, dialogue, lettering tests and Kindle readability tests should be developed in English first.

Tamil is deliberately deferred. The rejected first Tamil experiment is preserved for provenance but must not be polished into the final edition. When Tamil production begins, it should be a fresh literary adaptation based on:

1. the classical Tamil source;
2. the locked dramatic intent of the English edition;
3. the final page composition and available balloon/caption space;
4. independent Tamil literary editorial judgement.

The future Tamil edition may therefore differ from English in sentence shape, caption count, metaphor and balloon division while preserving narrative meaning.

## Source-first writing rule

The classical epic controls the chapter architecture.

For every chapter, distinguish four levels before polishing prose:

1. **registered primary-text detail** — may be paraphrased in the working English script but cannot be presented as exact translation until line-verified;
2. **cross-text/history support** — may shape setting and material detail without becoming a source claim about the canto;
3. **Bharathidasan perspective** — thematic lens only unless an individual use is separately verified and rights-cleared;
4. **graphic-novel interpretation** — original connective action, dialogue, framing or social juxtaposition.

When an interpretive page is taking space away from an important registered canto feature, the primary-text feature wins.

## Bharathidasan flavour

Use Bharathidasan's registered interpretive emphases as a creative lens, not as imitation-by-quotation.

Desired qualities in the English writing:

- concise, muscular sentences;
- lyrical contrast built from ordinary material objects;
- moral clarity without turning every caption into a sermon;
- sympathy for workers and people who are hungry, displaced, imprisoned or socially assigned;
- women stating decisions directly;
- questions that turn private feeling toward public responsibility;
- beauty allowed to be beautiful without being treated as consent;
- philosophical language that returns to lived consequences.

### English caption rhythm

Prefer:

> Puhar was still dark. The festival was already being built.

over explanatory narration that the image can carry.

Prefer:

> The drum promised abundance. Abundance is tested in smaller measures.

when a thematic contrast is anchored in a visible object such as a grain measure.

Prefer:

> One house did not answer the drum. By noon, the city would be speaking of its silence.

when a chapter handoff can create narrative pressure without invented character dialogue.

These are newly written project lines, not quotations from Bharathidasan or the classical epic.

### What “Bharathidasan flavour” does **not** mean

- translating Bharathidasan line by line into English;
- reproducing identifiable Venpa diction without verification/rights clearance;
- forcing rhyme or metre into balloons;
- inserting modern political slogans into ancient characters' mouths;
- making every working-class character deliver the project's thesis;
- reducing the epic's Buddhist/philosophical complexity to a single social message.

## English dialogue policy

Dialogue should be immediately readable but not sound casually twenty-first-century American.

- no faux-archaic “thee/thou” English;
- avoid fashionable slang and contemporary internet phrasing;
- use contractions only when they sound natural and do not trivialise tone;
- retain culturally specific Tamil names and concepts when the art/context can teach them;
- avoid explaining terms inside dialogue merely for the reader;
- let powerful characters speak economically;
- let ordinary workers have intelligence, humour and observation;
- avoid exposition that repeats what the art already shows;
- keep most balloons to one clear dramatic beat;
- prefer silence when an invented line only restates the scene's meaning.

### Caption policy

Use captions for:

- time/place transitions;
- compressed epic narration;
- registered source information that cannot be shown clearly;
- social or material contrast;
- interior reflection that cannot be drawn;
- chapter handoffs.

Avoid captions that merely describe the visible picture.

A caption should normally do at least one of three things:

1. change the meaning of the image;
2. connect two images across time/social position;
3. introduce information the art cannot contain.

### Source-paraphrase rule

Until the relevant Tamil lines are verified, any English rendering of registered epic content must be labelled internally as **paraphrase**. Do not use quotation marks in production notes in a way that implies a literal translation.

## Manimekalai

### Visual identity

At the beginning:

- young adult Tamil woman;
- trained physical poise inherited from an artistic household;
- observant rather than passive;
- adornment progressively simplified by choice, not as visual punishment for beauty;
- expressive eyes used for listening and assessment, not constant sorrow.

### Arc in framing

Part I: frequently framed through lattices, crowds, reflective surfaces and other people's sightlines.

Parts II–III: more solitary horizons and direct frontal decisions.

Parts IV–V: obstructed frames, doors and custody geometry.

Parts VI–VII: wider relational frames in which she sees networks of people, institutions, food and consequence.

## Madhavi

Never design her as a fallen-world stereotype.

She is:

- an accomplished artist;
- a worker within a prestige economy;
- a mother carrying grief and responsibility;
- someone whose renunciation does not erase skill, memory or economic consequences.

Her costumes may simplify, but the body still carries years of dance discipline.

## Chitrapathi

Avoid villain shorthand.

She understands:

- household survival;
- patronage;
- public reputation;
- the economic value and danger of artistic visibility.

The conflict is strongest when the reader can understand what she fears losing even while rejecting any attempt to assign Manimekalai's future.

## Sudhamathi

Design for practical intelligence and protective attention.

She should often notice danger or social movement one beat before others. Her body language is active: positioning, watching exits, interrupting trajectories, guiding without speaking over Manimekalai.

## Udayakumaran

He must be visually charismatic enough that the tragedy cannot be dismissed as obvious villainy.

Rules:

- desire is sincere;
- entitlement is still wrong;
- refusal is not flirtation;
- previous-life or karmic connection never becomes present-tense consent;
- his deterioration should be shown through choices, not grotesque visual coding.

## Puhar visual language

### Palette direction

- humid gold at dawn;
- indigo shade;
- dyed textile colour;
- wet timber, rope, grain, flower colour and river-silt neutrals;
- no generic fantasy glow.

### City rule

Every spectacular establishing image must contain visible evidence of labour.

If a procession is beautiful, someone prepared the street.  
If garlands hang, someone strung them.  
If ships carry wealth, someone repairs hulls and lifts cargo.  
If music sounds, someone trained, tuned and is paid—or waits to be paid.

### Chapter 1 source-visible rule

The first canto's registered features must be visible before decorative invention:

- fresh sand replacing old sand in public routes;
- proclamation of the twenty-eight-day festival;
- visitors / many voices in the port city;
- public philosophical plurality and debate;
- the link between festival, rain, prosperity and protection from famine/sickness/enmity.

## Sacred events

Sacred events may alter:

- continuity;
- scale;
- sound;
- horizon;
- repetition;
- reflections;
- memory order.

They should not rely on decorative beams of light as proof of holiness.

## Amudhasurabhi

Do not give the bowl a permanent magical aura.

Its extraordinary nature is discovered by consequence:

- food continues to be served;
- recipients remain individuals;
- serving labour continues;
- water, cleanliness, vessels, access and crowd management still matter;
- the miracle increases responsibility rather than removing logistics.

## Violence

Violence should be fast, legible and consequential.

Do not aestheticise Udayakumaran's death as romantic destiny. The panel after violence matters more than the strike: witness, blood, shock, misunderstanding and the state response.

## Philosophy pages

Use spatial design to differentiate arguments, not moral colour coding.

Examples:

- one school may reason through categorised objects;
- another through debate circles;
- another through ritual action;
- another through inference diagrams or teaching gestures.

All speakers should appear intellectually serious until their claims are tested.

## Panel grammar

### Standard page

- 4–5 panels;
- one visual anchor panel;
- one reaction panel;
- one consequence or transition panel.

### Splash page

Use only when the reader's understanding of scale changes.

Good uses:

- first full Puhar reveal;
- first Manipallavam reveal;
- first successful mass feeding;
- city-level famine contrast;
- final visual relinquishment.

### Close-up rule

A close-up should reveal a decision, recognition or withheld response—not merely decorate beauty.

## English lettering

### Balloons

- prefer short clauses;
- ideally 1–3 sentences;
- one emotional or argumentative beat per balloon when possible;
- break philosophical argument across actions and reactions;
- do not shrink font size to save an over-written balloon.

### Captions

- keep them substantially shorter than screenplay action;
- avoid stacking multiple narration boxes unless the page rhythm requires it;
- reserve a consistent visual hierarchy for chapter/title captions versus narration;
- write for the whole-page reader first and Guided View reader second, then test both.

### Deferred Tamil lettering

When Tamil begins later:

- choose a Tamil font with explicit commercial publishing rights;
- re-space balloons rather than forcing Tamil into English geometry;
- allow Tamil sentence order to reshape balloons/panel captions;
- conduct native literary copy editing before final lettering;
- test glyph integrity separately in every export path.

## Graphic continuity IDs

Use stable IDs from the pilot onward:

- `GN-CH01-P001` — page;
- `GN-CH01-P001-A` — panel;
- `GN-ASSET-0001` — artwork/reference asset;
- `GN-VENPA-USE-001` — perspective use;
- `GN-RIGHTS-001` — rights question;
- `GN-QA-001` — Kindle/lettering issue.

## Evidence footer during production

Working scripts may include production-only evidence notes below a page. These notes are never printed inside the story page.

Example:

`TRACE: primary canto 1 | EV-0002/EV-0004 | PERSPECTIVE VENPA-BHARATHIDASAN: THEMATIC | classification [TEXT]+[INTERPRETATION]`

## Prohibited shortcuts

- no generic North-Indian palace aesthetic substituted for evidence;
- no serpent/reptile racialisation of Naga identity;
- no modern saffron-versus-Buddhist colour binary;
- no divine glow as the default sign of goodness;
- no erotic framing that contradicts the story's consent logic;
- no crowd of identical anonymous poor people around the magic bowl;
- no text-heavy philosophy pages that become illustrated essays;
- no claim that generated architecture or costume is 'historically exact';
- no generated lettering embedded in art;
- no using the rejected Tamil v0.1 as future Tamil final copy;
- no downstream screenplay invention displacing a more important primary-canto feature without an explicit editorial decision.
