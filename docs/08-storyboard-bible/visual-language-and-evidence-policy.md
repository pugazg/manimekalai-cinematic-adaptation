# Visual Language and Evidence Policy

## Visual thesis

The film’s visual movement is not from colour to colourlessness or from worldly life to supernatural escape. It moves from being watched and assigned by others toward seeing causes, organising care and sharing responsibility.

The camera must remain answerable to bodies, labour, water, food, distance and institutions. Scale is valuable when it reveals relationships; spectacle is not evidence.

## Ten-sequence grammar

| Sequence | Visual pressure | Camera principle | Palette direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | City, labour and inherited identity | Human-height movement through connected work | Humid gold, indigo shade, working colour |
| 02 | Public gaze and pursuit | Exposed routes, obstructed exits, listening close-ups | Festival saturation tightening into risk |
| 03 | Memory and sacred discontinuity | Sparse horizons, physical vulnerability, discontinuous time | Pearl grey, salt white, shallow-water green |
| 04 | Hunger becoming organised service | Hands, vessels, water and access before miracle | Earthen food colours and inhabited courtyards |
| 05 | Recognition becoming false certainty | Partial sightlines and withheld spatial information | Gathering dusk and divided interiors |
| 06 | Grief becoming state action | Doors, records, delegated hands and constrained frames | Controlled lamplight and stone shadow |
| 07 | Listening and relinquishment | Distance opens as control is surrendered | Colour returns through witnesses, not absolution |
| 08 | Memory becoming governance | Fertility contrasted with remains and administrative work | Green, bone, sand and unornamented authority |
| 09 | Inquiry | Each school receives coherent space and attention | Neutral daylight; no winner’s halo |
| 10 | Shared practice | Networks of roads, water, workshops and serving lines | Depleted colour recovers through collective work |

## Shot rules

1. Every active feature unit receives three mandatory storyboard functions: entry, decision and consequence.
2. Reaction and listening precede explanatory dialogue where possible.
3. The frame must show who performs labour behind wealth, ritual, food and administration.
4. Refusal is staged as complete information, not romantic delay.
5. Hunger recipients retain individuality and reciprocal attention.
6. Sacred events may alter continuity, scale or sound, but must not erase physical consequence.
7. Amudhasurabhi does not emit decorative fantasy light. Its inexhaustibility is discovered through serving.
8. Philosophical rivals are not differentiated through grotesque or morally coded bodies.
9. Nāga identity is not represented through racialised or reptilian design.
10. No generated frame is labelled “historically exact.”

## Evidence labels

- `[TEXT]`: a primary literary detail.
- `[CROSS]`: a detail supported across early texts.
- `[HISTORY]`: archaeological, inscriptional or historical evidence.
- `[INTERPRETATION]`: the production’s synthesis.
- `[UNRESOLVED]`: a decision awaiting evidence or specialist review.

Every generated or commissioned frame remains `[INTERPRETATION]`, even when individual details are strongly evidenced.

## Generated-image controls

For every generated frame retain:

- asset ID and file hash;
- final prompt;
- generation tool and date;
- linked storyboard shot IDs;
- evidence and decision IDs;
- visible uncertainties;
- prohibited claims;
- human review status;
- rights/licensing status.

Generated images are internal concept studies until rights review and the relevant historical, costume, maritime or food specialist has cleared their use.
