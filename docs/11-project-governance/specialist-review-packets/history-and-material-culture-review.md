# History and Material Culture Review

## Scope

This packet covers `SR-006`, `SR-010`, `SR-011` and `SR-012`: early Tamil institutions, maritime archaeology, costume/textiles, ritual history and animal ethics.

## Evidence rule

Every visual or institutional conclusion must be labelled:

- `[TEXT]` — supported by a primary literary source;
- `[CROSS]` — supported across early texts;
- `[HISTORY]` — supported by archaeology, inscription or historical scholarship;
- `[INTERPRETATION]` — production reconstruction;
- `[UNRESOLVED]` — evidence is insufficient or disputed.

Comparative evidence establishes possibilities, not automatic Puhar-specific facts.

## Early institutions (`SR-006`)

Review custody, testimony, public records, prison conversion, local custodians, food storage, relief routes and public accountability.

Questions:

1. Which elements are directly literary?
2. Which are defensible functional reconstructions?
3. Which sound like modern bureaucracy projected backward?
4. Can public visibility and accountability be staged without claiming a documented historical office?
5. Are terms such as `வாக்குமூலம்` and `நிவாரணம்` acceptable performance vocabulary despite historical uncertainty?
6. Does institutional reform remain legible through action, water, food, access and witnesses rather than administrative speeches?

## Maritime world (`SR-010`)

Review Puhar’s distributed harbour, river landing, offshore transfer, vessel classes, cargo labour, pilots, seasonal travel, shipwreck and Manipallavam staging.

Required safeguards:

- no exact Puhar hull claim without evidence;
- no Roman-colonial-port framing;
- no transfer of Mantai, Alagankulam or wider Indian Ocean evidence as direct Puhar proof;
- no use of the later cannon wreck as ancient evidence;
- no claim that Nainativu/Nāgadīpa is archaeologically proven to be Manipallavam;
- no modern ethnic ownership claim derived from Nāga material.

Output should identify production-safe silhouettes, operations and weather behaviours, with confidence labels.

## Costume and textiles (`SR-011`)

Review clothing, drape, fibres, dyes, jewellery, hair, occupational wear, performance training, renunciant clothing and movement.

Required safeguards:

- no modern Bharatanatyam fan costume;
- no automatic medieval Chola jewellery package;
- no single “ancient Tamil costume” across occupation and status;
- no fixed Buddhist robe colour without evidence;
- no claim that archaeological ornaments reconstruct a complete garment;
- no use of adornment as consent or moral rank.

Provide separate guidance for evidence-supported materials, plausible construction and visibly interpretive choices.

## Ritual and animal ethics (`SR-012`)

Review Aputhiran’s cow and sacrifice sequence.

Questions:

1. Does the scene retain the literary ethical argument without claiming to document every historical practice?
2. Does the camera prioritise the animal’s fear and Aputhiran’s decision rather than spectacle?
3. Are community and ritual participants flattened into caricature?
4. Can all action be achieved without real distress, dangerous restraint or striking?
5. Does the cow’s escape avoid revenge comedy?

Animal-welfare planning is a production requirement, not only an interpretive note.

## Acceptance gate

This packet passes when every major design family has:

- a confidence label;
- a cited source trail;
- an explicit list of prohibited overclaims;
- production-safe implementation notes;
- unresolved matters carried forward rather than silently filled.
