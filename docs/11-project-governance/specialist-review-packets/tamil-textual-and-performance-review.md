# Tamil, Textual and Performance Review

## Scope

This packet covers `SR-001`, `SR-002`, `SR-003`, `SR-007`, `SR-008` and `SR-009`. It is designed for coordinated review by a Tamil screenplay/dialogue editor, classical-Tamil or *Manimekalai* scholar, Bharathidasan specialist, food historian, consent-sensitive dramaturg and trauma-informed performer.

The internal semantic and character-voice pass is complete. External reviewers are being asked to test and correct it, not to assume it is authoritative.

## Review A — Tamil dialogue and performance (`SR-001`)

Review every spoken Tamil line in order. For each concern, record:

- sequence and scene;
- speaker;
- current line;
- semantic issue, register issue or performance issue;
- proposed line;
- whether the English master also needs revision;
- expected timing or breath consequence.

Confirm or challenge:

1. action-line accuracy;
2. conversational rather than translated syntax;
3. distinct character voices;
4. consistency of honorifics and power relationships;
5. breath, interruption and actor speakability;
6. consistency between grief, philosophical and institutional registers;
7. the Tamil-script character-cue policy;
8. the locked `இராசமாதேவி` editorial form.

Deliverable: a scene-by-scene findings table plus an actor-led timing record. “Reads well silently” is not sufficient for approval.

## Review B — Bharathidasan perspective (`SR-002`)

Verify all implemented and deferred `VENPA-USE-*` anchors against a lawful scan or printed edition.

Required output:

- exact edition;
- printed page or folio where available;
- digital locator;
- corrected Tamil reading;
- surrounding verse movement;
- whether the project use is `DIRECT`, `ADAPTED`, `COMBINED` or `THEMATIC`;
- whether OCR affected any wording;
- whether the screenplay overstates Bharathidasan’s emphasis.

Special attention:

- Aadhirai’s prepared rice, accompaniment and ghee;
- Aputhiran’s service before reward;
- institutional and public-response motifs;
- Udayakumaran’s pursuit and Manimekalai’s grief;
- the distinction between Bharathidasan’s reinterpretation and Sāttanār’s epic.

## Review C — Primary *Manimekalai* compatibility (`SR-003`)

For every implemented `VENPA-USE-*` decision, classify the corresponding epic basis:

- `DIRECT_PRIMARY`;
- `COMPATIBLE_COMPRESSION`;
- `LATER_ELABORATION`;
- `SCREENPLAY_SYNTHESIS`;
- `CONFLICT_REQUIRES_REVISION`.

Check character names, episode order, branch-story causality, previous-birth material, Manimekalai’s refusal, Aadhirai’s offering, Kayasandihai’s post-hunger agency, Aputhiran’s final fast and the prison transformation.

No later perspective may silently overwrite a primary-source conflict.

## Review D — Food language and action (`SR-007`)

Review `சோறு`, `கறி`, `பசுநெய்`, food vessels, serving gestures, sequence, temperature, texture and household/public distribution.

State separately:

- what the primary epic supports;
- what Bharathidasan adds;
- what early-historic material evidence permits;
- what is production interpretation.

Do not infer a definitive ancient menu from one literary word. The accepted dramatic order is human preparation and giving first, inexhaustibility second, public logistics third.

## Review E — Gender, consent and trauma (`SR-008`, `SR-009`)

Test the following non-negotiable safeguards:

- beauty, previous birth, grief and persistence do not create consent;
- Manimekalai’s refusal remains complete;
- Udayakumaran’s humanity does not romanticise pursuit;
- Kanchanan retains responsibility for lethal inference;
- Manimekalai may grieve without inheriting guilt;
- Madhavi’s protection does not erase Manimekalai’s autonomy;
- Sudhamathi controls her testimony;
- Rajamadevi’s grief does not excuse state abuse;
- confinement, poisoning, defamation and starvation are not aestheticised;
- survivor characters retain ordinary life, humour and future agency.

The reviewer should identify both harmful framing and over-explicit corrective dialogue that weakens performance.

## Acceptance gate

This packet passes only when:

1. all ten sequences have named reviewer coverage;
2. every blocking correction is bilingual;
3. actor timing has been recorded;
4. terminology findings are entered in the controlled register;
5. source classifications are traceable;
6. validators pass after approved changes.
