# 04 — The Amudhasurabhi System

The bowl is the game's signature mechanic and its central ethical test. Its design
must satisfy one binding rule from the owner and the archive alike:

> **The bowl removes one form of scarcity while revealing the social systems that
> create hunger. The player must not succeed merely by selecting "Distribute food."**

Repository authority: `AD-0139` (the Amudhasurabhi does not erase distribution
labour and infrastructure); `04H` §6 Miracle rule (it does not create water,
vessels, safe queues, shelter, sanitation, transport, medical care, storage, fair
access); `RISK-011`; 10A "Compassion becomes logistics"; production plates
`amudhasurabhi-prop-continuity.md` and `food-vessel-and-service-workflow.md`.

## Design goal

Make the bowl **understandable, emotionally engaging and enjoyable** — *not* a
spreadsheet management sim (`GRT-05`). The system is **diegetic and spatial**: the
player arranges a handful of tangible things *in the scene* and reads results from
*people's behaviour*, not from bars and menus.

## What is infinite

- **Cooked/edible food quantity.** The vessel refills; "the level has not fallen"
  (`FU-022`). Food is never the bottleneck and can never be hoarded, sold, or run out.

That is the *only* infinite thing. Everything else is scarce.

## What remains scarce (the real game)

Grouped as the owner listed them; each is a *lever or constraint*, not a number to
babysit. In any scenario only **2–4** are the *binding* constraints — the ones that
must be solved for the scene to succeed.

| Scarce factor | In-world form | Owner list item |
|---|---|---|
| **Clean water** | A water source/route to establish | water, sanitation |
| **Cooking / serving** | Where food is ladled; who ladles | cooking, serving, vessels |
| **Vessels** | Bowls to eat from and to clean | vessels |
| **Serving hands / labour** | People to serve, clean, carry, translate | labour |
| **Safe crowd movement** | Queue routes; preventing a crush | crowd movement, safety |
| **Access** | Who can physically reach the food | access |
| **Disability & mobility** | Those who "cannot stand behind those who can" | disability & mobility barriers |
| **Excluded groups** | Migrants, prisoners, animals at the edge | prisoner access, migrant access, exclusion |
| **Recipient dignity** | Served *at their level*, not as a devotional crowd | recipient dignity |
| **Unequal power** | Guards, officials, owners who can block or seize | unequal power |
| **Sustainability & local ownership** | Does relief continue after you leave? | sustainability, local ownership |

Sources for the specific frictions: `FU-022` (water runs short, a child nearly
knocked down, translation for travellers, animals at the edge); `04H` §6 dignity
rule; `FU-024` prisoner access; `FU-023` "Enough" (dignity/satiety).

## Player inputs (the "arrange" verb, not a menu)

Per feeding scenario, the player has a **small set of placeable/assignable actions**
(target ≤ 5 visible at once), performed by walking to a spot and acting:

1. **Open a second line** ("Those who cannot stand do not wait behind those who
   can" — `FU-022`/Sudhamathi): splits a crush and prioritises by need, not speed.
2. **Establish a water route** (a water-carrier path): unlocks safe drinking + bowl
   washing (`FU-023` "Water. Rest.").
3. **Assign a hand** to a role: serving, bowl-cleaning, translating, carrying to
   those who can't approach. Roles are often *recipients themselves* becoming
   workers (`FU-022`: "A FORMER RECIPIENT begins cleaning bowls").
4. **Set an animal/edge boundary** so creatures are fed without disrupting people
   (`04H` §6; `FU-022`).
5. **Serve at eye level** (a dignity action — kneel to a recipient, as Manimekalai
   does for Kayasandihai, `FU-023`).

These are **spatial** decisions read off the scene, echoing the storyboard's
participant-height framing. The player is *organising a space*, closer to a light
Diner-Dash-of-conscience than to a strategy grid — but with no timer-driven fail and
no score.

## Social & logistical variables (state, mostly hidden from HUD)

Tracked internally, surfaced through behaviour and the journal — never as a bar row:

- **Crowd pressure** (crush risk) — shown as bodies pressing, not a meter.
- **Reach** — proportion of present people who *can physically get food*.
- **Dignity** — served-at-level vs. herded; surfaced as posture, the "Enough" beat.
- **Continuity** — will this run without Manimekalai? (a named local taking over).
- **Power interference** — an official's unease (`FU-024` royal official).

## Feedback to the player

Feedback is **embodied and specific** (Pillar 3), designed to be emotionally legible:

- **The "Enough" moment** (`FU-023`): a fed person's body changes — "her breath
  slows, her shoulders lower, she notices sound, shade and the people near her" —
  and *says* "Enough". This is the primary positive feedback, and it is dignity, not
  a score pop.
- **Unmet need stays visible:** the person who can't reach the line remains at the
  edge; unsafe water is shown; a near-crush replays until routed. The scene will not
  register success while a binding constraint is unmet (Pillar 3 acceptance test).
- **A system forming around her** (`FU-022`): when the player has arranged roles
  well, NPC workers begin acting autonomously — the visual payoff of "I thought the
  vessel was the work / The vessel is food. The work is everyone reaching it safely."
- **Journal reflection** (`GM-12`) names what is still unresolved after the scene.

## Failure and partial-success conditions

No hard fail. Outcomes are **graded by aftermath, not points** (see `05`
consequence axes):

- **Partial success** — food reached most, but (e.g.) a disabled elder never did, or
  the arrangement collapses once you leave. The scene proceeds; the aftermath card
  records the gap; it may resurface (a person you missed appears later).
- **Dignity failure** — everyone fed, but as a herded crowd; the "Enough" beat is
  denied; a recipient distrusts you next chapter (`GM-06`).
- **Sustainability failure** — you did it all yourself; nothing continues after
  departure (`RISK-013` decorative reform). The epilogue shows the space empty and
  the need returned.

## Dignity safeguards (binding)

- Recipients are **agents, not a devotional crowd** (`04H` §6; `AD-0138`). They can
  give information, correction, humour, help, and refusal.
- **No "gratitude" resource.** The player is never rewarded for being thanked; being
  needed is not the goal (`04H` central contradiction; `AD-0134`).
- **Consent & non-humiliation:** no recipient is fed by removing their agency; the
  game never frames poverty as spectacle (`RISK-011`; `RISK-006`).
- **The bowl is not a weapon or a status object** — no "denying food" verb exists;
  withholding food as coercion appears only as an *antagonist institution's* act in
  Act III (`SEQ-06`), which the player *counters*, never performs.

## How the system evolves over the game (scale, not complexity creep)

The bowl mechanic deepens by **changing the binding constraint**, not by adding
menus:

| Stage | Scene | New system wall the bowl reveals |
|---|---|---|
| **Household** | Aadhirai's threshold `FU-021` | Food must *cross a door*; private → public trust; women's authority (`AD-0168`) |
| **Public square** | Ulaga Aravi `FU-022`/`FU-023` | Crowd, water, queue safety, disability access, translation, animals, dignity ("Enough") |
| **Institution** | Prison kitchen `FU-024` | Power: who may enter, prisoner access, records, whether reform survives |
| **Later chapters** | Kanchi famine `SEQ-10` (`FU-057`–`FU-061`) | Water, crops, storage, price/access, 18-language access, *governance* — the bowl alone cannot feed a famine region; policy must |

By Sequence 10 the lesson is explicit and *earned by play*: "a responsibility that no
single owner can complete" (10A). The bowl never becomes more powerful — the
*world's* demands become more structural, forcing the player from serving to
organising to advocating for institutional change (`GM-10`).

## How it stays enjoyable rather than spreadsheet-heavy

1. **Low cardinality, high meaning:** ≤ 5 placeable actions per scenario; each maps
   to a visible human outcome.
2. **Read the room, not the UI:** success cues are bodies, posture, the "Enough"
   line — satisfying, humane, screenshot-worthy.
3. **One-shot arrangements, no upkeep loop:** you solve a space, then move on; no
   depleting bars.
4. **Emergent delight:** watching workers you empowered run the line themselves is
   the reward (`FU-022`).
5. **Difficulty scaling** (`12`): a "story" setting reduces constraint bookkeeping to
   the 1–2 most binding per scene while preserving the ethical beat.

## Anti-trivialisation checklist (must all hold in every feeding scene)

- [ ] "Distribute food" alone leaves visible unmet need and does not clear the scene.
- [ ] At least one constraint is *human/institutional* (access, dignity, power), not
      just logistical.
- [ ] At least one recipient becomes a worker/agent, not just a mouth.
- [ ] The "Enough"/dignity beat is reachable only via level-serving, not volume.
- [ ] The aftermath card can show *sustainability failure* if the player did it alone.
