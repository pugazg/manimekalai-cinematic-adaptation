# 05 — Agency and Canon (Bounded Agency)

The game uses **bounded agency**: the broad canonical arc is fixed; the player's
freedom is in *how* Manimekalai learns, listens, organises and reasons, and in the
*local* and *epilogue* consequences that follow. This preserves the archive's
"Non-negotiable principles" (10A) and its risk controls while making choice matter.

## The four-column model

Every beat in the game is tagged as one of:

- **FIXED** — canonical events that always happen (from `SEQ-*`/`FU-*`, 10A "Preserve").
- **VARIABLE METHOD** — how the player approaches a fixed beat.
- **VARIABLE OUTCOME (local / epilogue)** — what changes in the world and the ending
  cards along the consequence axes below.
- **PROHIBITED** — branches the game must never offer.

## Consequence axes (NOT a morality meter)

Per owner direction, there is **no good-vs-evil meter and no karma points**.
Outcomes are tracked on multiple independent, often-tensioned axes, surfaced in
aftermath/epilogue cards, never summed into one score:

`trust` · `access` · `dignity` · `public understanding` · `witness safety` ·
`hunger reduction` · `institutional reform` · `sustainability` · `unintended
effects` · `what happens after Manimekalai leaves`.

These can move in *opposite* directions in one choice (e.g. a fast intervention
raises hunger-reduction but lowers dignity and sustainability), which is the point —
there is no strictly-best line.

### VARIABLE METHOD (player choice; does not alter the fixed destination)

- **Whom Manimekalai listens to first** (e.g. Sudhamathi vs. a recipient vs. Aadhirai)
  — changes which evidence surfaces first and initial trust.
- **What evidence is discovered, and how it's classified** (`GM-04`) — thoroughness
  varies; some causes may be missed (fail-forward).
- **How relief is organised** (`GM-07`) — routes, roles, who is empowered.
- **How safely witnesses are heard** (`GM-06`) — approach that protects a witness vs.
  one that exposes them (critical in Act III).
- **Whether reforms are made durable** (records/handover) or left personal.
- **Order and framing of philosophical encounters** (`SEQ-09`) — which strong form
  the player reconstructs first.

### VARIABLE OUTCOME — local (this scene / chapter)

- Which people **trust** Manimekalai enough to give further testimony.
- Which vulnerable people are **reached** vs. left at the edge.
- Whether a recipient becomes a **local custodian** (sustainability).
- Whether an official **cooperates, obstructs, or later reverses** a reform.
- Whether a witness stays **safe** or is silenced (Act III stakes).

### VARIABLE OUTCOME — epilogue (persists to the ending)

The final "what remained unresolved" epilogue (`GM-12`) assembles the *aftermath
snapshots* the player accumulated:

- The **condition of each community** after she leaves (a fed square that sustained
  itself vs. one where need returned).
- **What communities remember** — the reform's memory survived, was erased, or
  became mere legend.
- Which relationships continued **without command** (Madhavi, per `10C`: "Ending
  allows continued relationship without command").
- The **institutional legacy** (did the care-standard hold or was it violated, as
  Rajamadevi later violates it per `10C`).

## How choices matter even though the destination is fixed

The game's answer to "why choose if the ending is set" is the epic's own answer:
**the meaning is in the practice and its aftermath, not in the plot's arrival.**

1. **Same event, different moral texture.** Udayakumaran still dies — but whether the
   player *protected witnesses*, *classified evidence honestly*, and *refused to
   treat resemblance as proof* changes who is safe and what the city understands
   afterwards. The tragedy is fixed; complicity, clarity and safety are not.
2. **The world remembers your method.** Trust/access/sustainability accrued in early
   chapters gate what's *possible* later (a community that trusts you helps convene
   witnesses in Act III). Method compounds into local outcome.
3. **The epilogue is authored by aggregation, not by a final choice.** No single
   "ending selector"; the closing state is the honest sum of aftermaths — which is
   why save-scumming a "good ending" is pointless (`03`).
4. **Correction is content.** Being wrong (misinference) and *accepting correction*
   is a designed, meaningful path (10A Kanchi lesson), not a fail.

## Communicating the boundary to the player (anti-frustration)

Because players *will* try to save Udayakumaran or "win" a debate, the game must
signal fixed vs. variable without breaking immersion:

- **[DESIGN]** A quiet diegetic framing: Manimekalai's own voice/journal marks what
  is beyond her power to change ("I could not have chosen his living; I can choose
  what is understood about his death"). This turns a design constraint into
  characterisation.
- The Ledger/journal visibly distinguishes *what I decided* from *what happened to
  me*, so players attribute tragedy correctly and never feel it was a skill failure
  (`03` fail-forward).
