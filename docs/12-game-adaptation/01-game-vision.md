# 01 — Game Vision

(e.g. *Project Amudhasurabhi*) until an owner + `SR-013` decision on public

## One-sentence premise

> A young woman is handed an inexhaustible alms-bowl and discovers that ending
> hunger is not an act but an organisation — of water, hands, access, testimony
> and power — that she must learn without ever raising a weapon.

## Player fantasy

Not "become powerful". The fantasy is **competent compassion**: *"I walked into a
place of suffering I did not cause, I listened until I actually understood it, and
I changed how living beings were treated there — knowing I could not fix
everything, and knowing I would have to leave."*

The player embodies Manimekalai's spine (10A): **refusal → revelation → ethical
obligation → service → catastrophic misinterpretation → inquiry → disciplined
reasoning → chosen practice**. The game lets the player *do* the service, *test*
the reasoning, and *feel* the misinterpretation — not just watch it.

## Emotional promise

- The relief of the word **"Enough"** — a body that stops fearing food will vanish.
- The dignity of being **listened to fully** before being helped (`FU-018`, "He Lets the Account Finish").
- The specific ache of **mistaken certainty** — that perception, memory and grief are not proof (10A Act II-B; `RISK-008`).
- The humility of leaving a place **improved but unfinished** ("what remains unresolved").

Explicitly **not** promised: triumph over villains, romance, a "correct" faith, or
a world made perfect.

## Intended audience

- **[DESIGN]** Primary: players of literary/"cosy-but-serious" narrative adventures
  (*Pentiment*, *Return of the Obra Dinn*'s deduction, *A Short Hike*'s traversal
  warmth, *Citizen Sleeper*'s systems-with-a-conscience), aged ~16+.
- Secondary: Tamil and Tamil-diaspora players seeking a serious cultural work in
  their language first (see `12`), and educators (ethics, epistemology, Classical
  Tamil literature).
- **Not** targeted: action/combat, gacha, hyper-casual, or "mythological power
  fantasy" audiences. Marketing must not promise those (`RISK-001`).

## Genre

**2D / 2.5D narrative adventure** combining:
exploration + conversation · social investigation · light public-service and
logistics simulation · Tamil-first bilingual cultural experience.

No combat. No inventory-crafting economy. No open world.

## Platform assumptions

**[ASSUMPTION]** Owner works in web technologies (confirmed context), and the
target players include Tamil-diaspora on **iPhone / iPad / Mac** (owner's own
platform). Therefore:

- **Vertical slice:** browser-first (runs on Safari/Chrome on Mac, iPad, iPhone;
  trivially shareable by URL for playtesting; strongest bilingual/text stack).
- **Later commercial version:** engine decision deferred to `10` and gate G7; a
  browser build can remain a first-class target or a marketing demo.
- Input: touch (iPad/iPhone), mouse+keyboard (Mac/desktop), with controller as a
  later-stage nicety (`12`).

## Estimated full-game length

- **Full game:** ~**8–12 hours** across the ten sequences (uneven by design — see
  `06`; not every `SEQ-*` gets equal playtime).
- **First milestone (vertical slice):** ~**45–60 minutes**, Sequence 04 only.
- The full game is **not** the first milestone and must not be scoped as such
  (`GRT-01` scope explosion; `RISK-015`).

## What makes this game distinctive

1. **The bowl removes one scarcity to reveal the system.** Infinite food is the
   *start* of the problem, not the solution — you still face water, vessels,
   cooking, serving, sanitation, access, labour, dignity (`AD-0139`; `RISK-011`).
   No other "feed the hungry" game makes abundance the *inciting revelation of
   structural hunger*.
2. **An epistemics mechanic drawn from the epic itself.** Manimekalai's arc is
   literally about perception, testimony, inference and proof (10A Act III;
   `05B`; `PHL-02`). The game's core investigation verb — classifying what you
   know into observation / testimony / inference / assumption / memory /
   philosophical claim / public proof — is not a bolt-on; it is the epic's own
   theory of knowledge turned into play.
3. **Nonviolent tension.** Danger is real (pursuit, coercion, starvation as a
   weapon in Act III) but the player never resolves it with force — only with
   listening, evidence, access and institutional pressure.
4. **Consequences after you leave.** The game tracks the *aftermath* of relief and
   reform (sustainability, local ownership), directly answering the film's final
   concern that the vessel "no single owner can complete".
5. **Tamil-first, evidence-honest.** It refuses false archaeological/geographic
   certainty (`RISK-002`/`003`/`005`) and philosophical caricature (`RISK-014`),
   turning the archive's cautions into design constraints, not disclaimers.

## Explicit non-goals

Restating the owner's creative direction as binding vision constraints:

- **Not** a generic mythological action game.
- **Not** a combat-focused RPG. There is **no** combat system, weapon tree, or
  enemy-defeat loop.
- **Not** a visual novel of screenplay scenes + dialogue choices only.
- **Not** a religious-propaganda game; no faith is the "winning" answer (10A
  non-negotiable #12; `05B`).
- **Not** an open-world project. Locations are authored, bounded neighbourhoods.
- **Not** a game where the Amudhasurabhi auto-solves problems. Selecting
  "Distribute food" alone must never succeed (`04`).
- **Not** a power fantasy, a romance with Udayakumaran, or a "prevent every
  tragedy" wish-fulfilment (`05`).
- **Not** a spreadsheet management sim — the logistics are diegetic and spatial,
  not menu-driven (`03`, `04`; `GRT-05`).
