# Animal Action and Welfare Plan 0.1

## Status and non-negotiable boundary

This plate implements the first `PD-012` production plan. It is an
`[INTERPRETATION]` grounded in the Aputhiran dossier, the historical-world
foundation and the bilingual screenplay. It is not veterinary advice, a
permit, an animal-use approval or a final reconstruction of early ritual.
`SR-012` requires joint animal-welfare and ritual-history review before lock.

No shot requires real fear, exhaustion, deprivation, striking, dangerous
restraint, forced crowd exposure, collision or animal-to-performer contact.
Historical importance never overrides welfare.

## Ethical and dramatic principles

1. The cow who responds to the abandoned infant is a caregiver, not a sacred
   special effect.
2. The restrained cow episode centres Aputhiran reading an animal's fear and
   choosing intervention; it does not require producing fear.
3. The escape and injury are consequences of human actions, not revenge comedy.
4. Festival and working animals reveal labour, care, heat, water and crowd
   pressure rather than royal scale alone.
5. Feeding includes animals and birds without mixing them into unsafe human
   food-service paths or creating a fantasy menagerie.
6. Absence is preferable to unsafe authenticity. A planned animal may be
   replaced by prop, animatronic, compositing, sound or human reaction.

## Action risk classes

| Class | Action type | Default method | Approval requirement |
|---|---|---|---|
| `A0 — implied presence` | sound, tracks, shadow, empty tack, feed/water aftermath | production sound, prop, VFX or plate | art/VFX continuity and species plausibility review |
| `A1 — calm controlled presence` | standing, slow walking, eating or resting in an open controlled area | trained animal with handler, short takes and escape route | welfare supervisor; veterinary plan; legal permissions; weather limits |
| `A2 — separated interaction` | performer and animal appear related but do not touch or share danger | separate plates, eyeline match, barrier or controlled distance | previsualisation; handler marks; composite review |
| `A3 — simulated distress or restraint` | narrative requires fear, tethering, exhaustion or injury | animatronic/prop inserts, breakaway equipment, sound and performer reaction; calm animal plates only | welfare and ritual-history approval of shot decomposition; no live distress |
| `A4 — simulated impact or escape` | goring, collision, charging or dangerous crowd movement | stunt performer, mechanical/prop animal component, VFX and editorial assembly | stunt, VFX and welfare sign-off; live animal excluded from impact setup |
| `A5 — prohibited live action` | deprivation, striking, forced fall, dangerous restraint, infant exposure, uncontrolled birds/crowd, harmful cosmetic or drug use | do not perform | cannot be waived by creative or historical preference |

## Sacrificial-cow shot decomposition

The sequence must be designed as separate safe components:

| Plate | Content | Live animal? | Safety boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| `COW-01` | wide geography with ritual participants and animal position | optional calm animal at protected distance | no weapons, fire, crowd compression or restraint pressure near animal |
| `COW-02` | Aputhiran notices body signals | no shared physical interaction required | performer eyeline to marker; animal plate captured separately |
| `COW-03` | eye, ear, breath, foot and decorated tether details | calm observational inserts or animatronic | do not provoke reactions; decoration pre-approved for weight, noise and contact |
| `COW-04` | tether and knot | prop/animatronic area | breakaway mechanism; never tighten on live neck or limb |
| `COW-05` | Aputhiran reaches and releases | prop tether and performer | no live animal within release/stunt arc |
| `COW-06` | cow crosses open route | calm trained walking plate | handler beyond crop; non-slip surface; take and temperature limits |
| `COW-07` | attacker moves to strike | performer/stunt only | weapon never present near animal |
| `COW-08` | impact/goring if included | mechanical/prop element, stunt and VFX | no live animal; horns flexible or breakaway; medical/stunt plan |
| `COW-09` | escape and aftermath | separate calm animal plate plus human consequence | do not chase, startle or celebrate the animal's fear |

Editorial assembly may create danger only after every live-animal plate is
complete and welfare-approved.

## Infant and caregiver-cow plan

- Use a realistic infant prop or animatronic for any lick, nuzzle, nursing or
  body-contact frame.
- Film any human infant separately under child-performance rules with no live
  animal sharing the action area.
- Do not withhold food from a calf/cow or manipulate maternal separation to
  obtain vocalisation.
- Use recorded licensed sound, handler-cued calm attention and editorial
  response rather than distress calls.
- Milk imagery may use food-safe practical inserts; never force nursing action.
- The seven-day duration is conveyed through safe repeated motifs and human
  discovery, not repeated animal-contact setups.

## Festival elephants and horses

The text and historical dossier allow ceremonial and occupational presence,
but do not require a live mass procession.

Preferred order:

1. show keepers, water, feed, shade, tack preparation and cleared routes;
2. use distant or separately photographed controlled animal plates;
3. extend numbers digitally only after real welfare relationships are visible;
4. use crowd sound and reaction without surrounding live animals;
5. omit or reduce the animal if safe conditions cannot be guaranteed.

No live elephant or horse is placed among uncontrolled extras, pyrotechnics,
drums at harmful proximity, slippery wet-down, hot reflective surfaces or
blocked exits. Decorative tack must be weight-, fit-, noise- and heat-reviewed.

## Public feeding separation

Animal and bird care uses the `Z9` edge defined in the PD-007 workflow:

- distinct vessels and implements;
- species-appropriate food and water;
- handler/keeper control;
- distance from cooking, clean tools, washing and human eating;
- no public petting or crowd convergence;
- waste and cleaning route that does not cross potable water;
- no repeated feeding solely to obtain extra takes;
- no uncontrolled release of birds or attraction of wildlife.

## Welfare authority and stop-work power

Before any live-animal work, production must name:

- legally required approving authority and permits;
- independent welfare supervisor;
- attending or on-call veterinarian as required;
- species-experienced handler;
- stunt/VFX lead for separated action;
- unit safety lead;
- transport, rest, weather and emergency plan.

The welfare supervisor, veterinarian and handler each have stop-work authority.
Creative leadership cannot overrule a welfare stop. A stopped shot moves to the
approved prop/VFX alternative.

## Behavioural stop indicators

The species expert defines specific thresholds. General stop indicators include
repeated avoidance, escape attempts, freezing outside trained behaviour,
agitation, abnormal vocalisation, respiratory stress, loss of footing,
overheating, refusal caused by pressure or any handler concern. These signs are
not useful “performance.” They end or modify the setup.

## Costume, makeup and prop restrictions

- No harmful dyes, adhesives, glitter, oils, smoke residue or unapproved paint
  on animals.
- No heavy, sharp, noisy, restrictive or heat-trapping decoration.
- No false wound, blood or emaciation makeup on a live animal unless explicitly
  cleared as harmless—and the default is prop/VFX instead.
- No weapon, flame, breakaway debris or stunt performer enters the live-animal
  safety zone.
- No tether attached to a live animal is used as the visual mechanism of force.

## Camera and sound rules

- Long lenses and separated eyelines create proximity; physical proximity is
  not an authenticity requirement.
- Do not hide handler access in a way that removes the actual exit or oversight.
- Distress sound must come from licensed sound design, not provoked behaviour.
- Slow motion, triumphant scoring and repeated impact angles are prohibited for
  the escape/goring movement.
- Reaction shots should identify who created danger and who chose intervention.
