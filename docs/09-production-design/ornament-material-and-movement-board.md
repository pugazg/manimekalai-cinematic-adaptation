# Ornament Material and Movement Board 0.1

**Control family:** `PD-006`

**Specialist gate:** `SR-011` — early historic textiles, ornament and dress

**Status:** `[INTERPRETATION]` — **MATERIAL POSSIBILITY IS NOT A COMPLETE COSTUME SET**

## Purpose

This board converts archaeological material categories and literary ornament cues into controlled prop tests. It does not assign a fixed gender, rank, body position or ritual meaning to an excavated object without evidence.

## Material families

| Code | Material possibility | Required surface treatment | Claim prohibited |
|---|---|---|---|
| `O1` | shell / conch | growth lines, cutting, polish and wear | plastic-white uniform bangles |
| `O2` | glass | colour and size variation, repair/loss logic | modern perfect repeat sets |
| `O3` | terracotta / clay | firing variation and edge wear | poverty marker by itself |
| `O4` | stone / carnelian / quartz | drilling and workmanship differences | fixed ethnicity inferred from bead type |
| `O5` | copper / base metal | oxidation, skin contact and maintenance | universal low-status assignment |
| `O6` | gold / gold wire | rarity, skilled manufacture and guarded storage | all-gold elite population |
| `O7` | flower / fibre / cord | freshness, decay, replacement and labour | permanent decorative perfection |
| `O8` | mixed / inherited set | repair, mismatch and personal continuity | standardized temple-jewellery package |

## Prop record

Every prominent ornament must record:

- `P1` evidence source and uncertainty class;
- `P2` material and fabrication hypothesis;
- `P3` wearer, scene and narrative function;
- `P4` attachment and release method;
- `P5` movement and snag risk;
- `P6` sound signature;
- `P7` duplicate/stunt/safety version;
- `P8` continuity state: new, maintained, worn, removed, repaired, sold or lost.

## Movement and sound tests

| Test | Required result |
|---|---|
| `T1 — wrist` | shell, glass and metal remain audibly distinct |
| `T2 — turn` | earrings and hair elements stay safe and narratively legible |
| `T3 — floor` | no unsafe impact, snag or hidden modern dance assumption |
| `T4 — work` | ornament does not prevent the occupation being shown |
| `T5 — crowd` | emergency removal and duplicates are documented |
| `T6 — water/salt` | finish and continuity respond plausibly |
| `T7 — removal` | reduced ornament changes sound and gaze without moral judgement |
| `T8 — close camera` | drilling, wear and handmade variation survive scrutiny |

## Narrative controls

- Manimekalai’s bangles are a sound and agency motif, not a consent signal.
- Madhavi’s stored ornaments carry artistic labour and household economy, not “fallen woman” coding.
- Chitrapathi’s maintained pieces show institutional continuity and investment.
- Royal ornament shows guarded access and workmanship, not fantasy mass.
- Working people may possess inherited or modest ornament; poverty is not visual nudity.
- Renunciation may reduce or repurpose objects without declaring material culture immoral.

## Binding prohibitions

- no uniform modern temple-jewellery package;
- no perfect machine-identical bead strings unless elite specialist manufacture is specifically justified;
- no poetic pearl, coral or gold comparison automatically made literal;
- no excavated material transferred item-for-item from another site into Puhar;
- no ornament treated as proof of consent, virtue, sexuality, caste or ethnicity;
- no all-gold court or all-unornamented labouring population;
- no “exact ancient jewellery” publicity claim.

## Approval path

Produce material samples for `O1`–`O8`, complete `P1`–`P8` records and `T1`–`T8` tests, then obtain `SR-011` review of chronology, fabrication, terminology and wear.
