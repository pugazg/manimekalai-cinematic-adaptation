# Music, Performance and Acoustic Evidence Dossier 0.1

**Control family:** `PD-009`

**Specialist gates:** `SR-001` — Tamil dialogue/performance; `SR-003` — religion and philosophy

**Status:** `[INTERPRETATION]` — **NO EXACT ANCIENT ENSEMBLE OR MODERN CLASSICAL-STAGE DEFAULT**

## Purpose

This dossier separates sound arising inside the story world from composed film score. It protects performance as skilled labour and prevents one generic devotional or “ancient India” sound from covering festival, household, harbour, debate, grief, service and famine.

## Evidence rule

Every prominent instrument, vocal form or movement vocabulary must be classified as:

- `[TEXT]` — named or clearly functioning in the primary epic;
- `[CROSS]` — supported by a controlled early Tamil comparison;
- `[HISTORY]` — supported by period-relevant material or scholarship;
- `[INTERPRETATION]` — created for dramatic function;
- `[OPEN]` — terminology, construction, technique or chronology unresolved.

The proclamation drum is textually functional. That does not authorize a complete exact drum construction, rhythm, elephant mounting system or festival ensemble without specialist review.

## Performance labour chain

Madhavi’s world requires visible and audible work:

1. `R1` instruction and correction;
2. `R2` individual practice;
3. `R3` ensemble coordination;
4. `R4` costume and ornament adjustment;
5. `R5` instrument care, tuning and repair;
6. `R6` flower, floor, light and audience preparation;
7. `R7` patronage, accounts and dependent livelihoods;
8. `R8` recovery, storage and knowledge transmission after performance.

The house’s withdrawal from the festival therefore changes income, routine, rehearsal, instrument use and worker futures. It is not communicated merely by closing a decorative dance hall.

## Instrument-control record

Every hero instrument or sound object requires:

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| `I1` | textual/historical anchor and uncertainty class |
| `I2` | regional and chronological review |
| `I3` | material and construction hypothesis |
| `I4` | holding, striking, blowing or sounding technique |
| `I5` | who may learn, own, carry, repair and hear it |
| `I6` | acoustic test in the actual set volume |
| `I7` | performer safety, animal proximity and crowd limits |
| `I8` | distinction between source sound, designed enhancement and score |

No named ancient instrument may be rebuilt from a modern stage version and then marketed as exact merely because the name survives.

## Movement control

- Manimekalai retains balance, rhythm and spatial awareness from the artistic household without performing a modern codified dance vocabulary.
- Madhavi’s body retains training after renunciation; stillness is controlled action, not loss of skill.
- Sudhamathi’s movement is practical, route-aware and responsive rather than companion choreography.
- Debate speakers occupy space through voice, audience and institutional position—not stylized villain blocking.
- Feeding movement is repetitive skilled service with fatigue, sanitation and recipient dignity.
- Philosophical or sacred scenes may alter rhythm, but actors remain embodied and interruptible.

## Recording and reconstruction tests

1. `A1` — dry set impulse and voice intelligibility;
2. `A2` — harbour/wind competition test;
3. `A3` — drum reach without artificial dominance;
4. `A4` — ornament, foot and cloth separation;
5. `A5` — debate audience and multilingual speech clarity;
6. `A6` — feeding workflow sound without miracle fanfare;
7. `A7` — silence and low-level work in Madhavi’s house;
8. `A8` — violence mix preserving choice and consequence;
9. `A9` — famine-to-relief change through work returning gradually;
10. `A10` — score mute test: the scene’s causality must remain legible without music.

## Binding prohibitions

- no generic devotional score for every religious community;
- no modern Carnatic concert or Bharatanatyam orchestra treated as direct ancient evidence;
- no single “tribal,” “folk” or “mystical” texture used for difference;
- no rival philosophical school assigned villain music;
- no romantic theme rewarding persistent pursuit;
- no heroic action cue for Kanchanan’s killing;
- no triumphant cue rewarding Aputhiran’s self-erasure;
- no sacred drone that removes hunger, fatigue, fear or consequence;
- no exact instrument, rhythm, tuning or ensemble claim without evidence;
- no continuous orchestral layer masking Puhar’s labour ecology.

## Approval path

1. Extract and concordance exact Tamil music, instrument, gesture and performance terms.
2. Review comparative early Tamil evidence without projecting later codification backward.
3. Build provisional `I1`–`I8` records and conduct `A1`–`A10` tests.
4. Obtain `SR-001` spoken/performance and `SR-003` religious/philosophical review.
5. Run actor, musician, sound, movement and animal-welfare rehearsals before score lock.

Until those gates close, this dossier controls dramatic and acoustic function—not an exact historical soundtrack.
