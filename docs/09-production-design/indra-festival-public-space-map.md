# Indra Festival Public-Space Map 0.1

**Control family:** `PD-002`

**Specialist gates:** `SR-003` — religion and philosophy; `SR-006` — early Tamil institutions

**Status:** `[INTERPRETATION]` — **NOT A RECONSTRUCTED RITUAL ITINERARY**

## Foundational rule

The festival is staged first as the twenty-eight-day civic-sacral institution described within *Manimekalai* and related early Tamil evidence. It is connected with royal proclamation, rain, prosperity, famine prevention, public renewal, trade, ceremony, teaching, regulated disagreement and social celebration.

It must not be presented as automatically identical to later Purāṇic, medieval, colonial, calendar-art or modern devotional representations of Indra. The opening does not require an embodied Indra.

## Purpose

This map controls movement between public functions. It is not an exact Puhar street plan or a claim that the listed events formed one archaeologically recovered procession.

## Public-space zones

| Code | Zone | Screen function | Required counter-image |
|---|---|---|---|
| `F1` | River and harbour labour | arrivals, cargo, sediment, water and festival supply | risk, fatigue and goods that do not reach everyone |
| `F2` | Renewal streets | old sand removed, fresh sand spread, façades and thresholds prepared | cleaners and carriers visible before elite arrival |
| `F3` | Royal-civic proclamation route | heralds, drum, officers and public obligations | authority shown as responsibility, not divine ownership |
| `F4` | Market and merchant quarter | increased exchange, visitors, food and ornament | price, access, repair and hidden scarcity |
| `F5` | Ceremonial animal corridor | controlled elephant and horse presence | welfare space, handlers and escape routes |
| `F6` | Debate and teaching court | plural teachers, audiences and rules against violent quarrelling | disagreement without one uniform theology |
| `F7` | Performance and rehearsal quarter | musicians, trained artists and dependent labour | Madhavi’s absent household and lost work |
| `F8` | Household threshold network | lamps, leaves, flowers, alms and family participation | unequal household capacity and private grief |
| `F9` | Marginal and care edge | those excluded from visible abundance | hunger as the festival’s moral contradiction |

## Screen-time phases

These phases create cinematic order; they do not claim an exact ancient daily schedule.

| Phase | Movement | Sound transition |
|---|---|---|
| `P0 — Before dawn` | `F1 → F2` | surf, wheels, sweeping, water jars, animal breath |
| `P1 — Proclamation` | `F3` crosses working streets | drum and herald voice enter existing labour sound |
| `P2 — Public transformation` | `F2 → F4 → F8` | tools, merchant calls, rehearsal and children |
| `P3 — Civic plurality` | `F6` and adjacent routes | argument, recitation, audience response, rules of order |
| `P4 — Ceremonial pressure` | `F5` intersects `F3/F4` | handlers and crowd control remain audible beneath ceremony |
| `P5 — Refusal and absence` | `F7 → F8` at Madhavi’s household | festival sound becomes distant and incomplete |
| `P6 — Moral counter-route` | `F4/F8 → F9` | celebration thins into hunger, care and ordinary speech |

## Proclamation-drum control

- The drum announces a civic obligation and a twenty-eight-day festival.
- Its route must pass workers, merchants, teachers, households and people outside abundance.
- An elephant may carry it only under the animal-welfare plan; the animal is not a decorative platform.
- The drum does not prove a specific later icon, vehicle, temple or theology.
- Repetition may mark distance and public reach, but it cannot become uninterrupted triumphal scoring.

## Twenty-eight-day caution

The textual duration is retained. Production may show changing crowd, maintenance and market states across the period, but it may not invent a complete day-by-day ritual calendar and label it historically exact without line-level and specialist support.

## Crowd and access rules

1. Every ceremonial route has handler, water, shade, emergency and crossing space.
2. Workers are present before decoration and remain after celebration.
3. Debate listeners differ by occupation, language, status and commitment.
4. Teachers are distinct without being converted into modern denominational uniforms.
5. Military or royal presence does not erase merchants, women, children, disabled people or marginal residents.
6. Abundance and exclusion appear in the same geographic system rather than separate “rich” and “poor” montages.
7. Crowd density never blocks real animal-welfare and performer-safety corridors.

## Visual and iconographic prohibitions

- no automatic later calendar-art Indra figure;
- no massive medieval temple complex as the festival centre;
- no uniform “Hindu festival” label or theology;
- no single temple procession replacing the whole city;
- no modern interfaith-conference staging of philosophical debate;
- no fantasy celestial spectacle used to avoid civic labour;
- no ornamental animal crowding or distress;
- no uninterrupted spectacle that conceals hunger and exclusion;
- no claim that the route diagram is an exact ancient map.
